## 1. What Zimmy is (the positioning the site must express)

From the Strategy and Product Vision tabs:

- **Core promise:** predictable outcomes from creator content, powered by extensive AI research. The research is the long-term moat, and the tooling sits on top of it.
- **The ladder:** AI UGC → UGC → Ads → Influencer marketing. Find winners cheaply with AI UGC, remake them with real creators, then scale the proven ones as paid ads.
- **For brands (P0):** campaign success through predictable campaign design. Objectives: AI UGC on autopilot, turning winning AI UGC into real UGC, and turning winning UGC into ads. Success means virality, reach and conversion at a low CPM.
- **For creators (P1):** views and audience growth through a content calendar built on what is already working, plus brand deals and other income.
- **Human in the loop:** an AI agent does the work and a person reviews it at key steps, so it keeps getting better.
- **Full-funnel view with insights**, with a sheets-like ease of use.
- **Global narrative, local relevance:** research covers each market (India, EU, English-speaking markets), not just the US.

### The workflow (use this as the site's "how it works" spine)

| Step | Strategy tab | What the site should say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Define the marketing AHA moment | Find the moment someone sees the product and gets it |
| 1 | Define category / content niche | Pick the niche and audience |
| 2 | Research virality patterns in that niche, per market | Find outlier videos in each market |
| 3 | Decode the "why" (human in the loop) | Break down the hook, sequence and payoff; a person checks the reasoning |
| 4 | Prioritised content experiment board | A ranked board of what to test |
| 5 | Create AI UGC videos | Generate test videos |
| 6 | Post across platforms | Publish to TikTok, Reels, Shorts and others |
| 7 | Live analytics | Read results per platform |
| 8 | Decide the next experiments within budget | Double down, remake with real creators, or drop |

## 2. Insights from the article (paraphrased)

1. **Research comes before generation.** "Make me a viral video" fails. The AI needs who the product is for, what problem it solves and what a viewer can *see* that makes the benefit obvious. Garbage in, garbage out.
2. **Outliers beat raw views.** A video doing many times better than that account's usual views is a stronger signal than a big view count. An outlier is a reason to investigate, not proof.
3. **Make research usable.** Turn findings into a visual board of cards showing the source, its metrics, why it was picked and a select button.
4. **Reverse-engineer the why.** Look at the hook, the open question, the sequence and the payoff. A caption and a view count aren't enough.
5. **Change one thing at a time** (hook, character, setting, format, length, product placement, CTA) so results can be read.
6. **Believability is designed, not prompted.** Camera angle, light, setting, hands and sound are deliberate choices.
7. **Show the real product.** Use real app footage. Never let a model invent an interface the product doesn't have.
8. **Judge each platform on its own.** A weak TikTok result doesn't cancel a strong Reels one. The questions: did the opening earn attention, did people understand the product, did it lead to action, and is it repeatable enough to fund?
9. **AI is the testing ground; real creators are the scale.** Remake proven concepts with real creators for trust, then earn the right to spend on paid ads.

## 3. Keep / drop rules

**Keep** content that:
- maps to a workflow step or a brand or creator objective above
- states a mechanism ("we find videos beating their account's baseline") rather than an adjective ("cutting-edge AI")
- can be checked: the product really does it today, or it is clearly marked as coming soon
- shows a human checkpoint wherever the AI makes a judgement call

**Drop** content that:
- names a company as a client, or quotes a testimonial, unless that customer has agreed to it in writing. Eddy Finance, Supatest, Agnost AI, Docterz and Novelty were POCs and must never appear.
- uses invented results such as "12× faster" or "+128%", unless they come from a real, documented campaign
- promises guaranteed virality. The honest claim is *more predictable*, not *guaranteed*.
- copies a competitor's wording, layout or illustration. Borrow the **pattern**, then write it in Zimmy's own words.
- shows a fake product interface presented as real. Mock-ups must be labelled as illustrative.

**Voice:** plain English, short sentences, operator-to-operator. Concrete numbers only when they're real. No jargon without a one-line explanation (for example "outlier: a video doing far better than that account usually does").

## 4. What to collect from a competitor site

For each site in the Similar Startups tab, capture:

- **Positioning:** one line in our own words, plus who they target.
- **Section order:** the sequence of sections from top to bottom.
- **Elements worth borrowing:** cards, interactive demos, calculators, galleries, comparison tables, pricing layouts, proof formats. Describe each pattern and why it works.
- **Proof strategy:** how they earn trust (numbers, logos, video examples, case studies) and whether we can do the same honestly.
- **Gaps:** what they don't say that Zimmy can own (research moat, human in the loop, multi-market, AI UGC → real UGC → ads).
- **Change over time,** if visible: archived versions, "new" badges, changelogs.

Quote at most a short phrase when needed to identify a pattern. Never copy paragraphs.
